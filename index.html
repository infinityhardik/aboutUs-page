Which Grade for Which Room?

How much water a board will see decides the grade it needs. Pick a room to see the board we would put there — and why.

Same water, different bond. MR plywood is bonded for everyday humidity; BWP is phenol-bonded to take standing water without the plies letting go.

Water exposure: low

Bedrooms, living rooms and wardrobes

Use MR grade plywood, block board and flush doors.

Dry rooms only see everyday humidity, so moisture-resistant (MR, IS 303) bonding is enough — and it costs less than BWP. Spend the difference on thickness where it matters: 18mm for carcasses and shelves that carry load.

  • Commercial MR plywood
  • Pine wood block board
  • Pine wood flush doors

Water exposure: high — steam, splashes, the sink

Modular kitchens

Use BWP / IS 710 plywood below the counter, HDHMR for shutters.

Steam and splashes reach the carcass every day, and the sink unit gets standing water. Build base units and the sink cabinet from 100% BWP marine plywood to IS 710; for shutters, Motherwood Super HD+WR (HDHMR) takes laminate or paint and resists moisture. MR grade is only for tall units away from the hob and sink.

  • Marine plywood IS 710
  • Motherwood Super HD+WR
  • HDHMR boards

Water exposure: direct — splashing and standing water

Bathrooms and wet areas

Use 100% BWP flush doors and BWP block board.

A bathroom door is splashed and steamed every day; an MR door's core swells and its skin lifts. Fit a 100% BWP (phenol-bonded) flush door, seal every edge including the bottom, and build vanities from BWP block board or marine plywood.

  • BWP waterproof flush doors
  • Waterproof bathroom doors
  • BWP waterproof block board

Water exposure: high — monsoon humidity, wind-driven rain

Balconies and covered semi-outdoor spaces

Use BWP marine plywood and Exterior Grade MDF.

Monsoon humidity and wind-driven rain reach covered balconies and verandas. Use BWP / IS 710 marine plywood for furniture and Motherwood Exterior Grade MDF for covered panels, and finish every face and edge — no board here is meant for open, unprotected weather.

  • Marine plywood IS 710
  • Motherwood Exterior Grade MDF
  • Interior vs exterior MDF

Use 100% BWP marine plywood to IS 710 for base cabinets, the sink unit and anything that will see water or steam. MR grade plywood is fine for dry wardrobes, beds and TV units. For carcasses 18mm is the working thickness, and 6mm is enough for back panels.

BWP (Boiling Water Proof) is the higher grade. BWP plywood is bonded to withstand prolonged exposure to water and is the grade for kitchens and bathrooms, while BWR (Boiling Water Resistant) suits areas with only mild humidity. We supply BWP marine plywood and BWP flush doors for wet areas.

MDF (Medium Density Fibreboard) is made from wood fibres bonded with resin under heat and pressure. Unlike plywood it has a smooth, uniform surface with no grain or knots, which makes it ideal for painting, routing and precision shutters, while plywood is stronger for load-bearing carcasses. Motherwood grades run, from entry to top: Interior, Exterior, Super HD+WR (HDHMR) and TurBo+. Compare them on the Motherwood MDF page.

HDHMR stands for High Density High Moisture Resistant board, a denser, more moisture-resistant category of fibreboard. Motherwood's Super HD+WR is an HDHMR-grade board, stocked in 3, 5.5, 7.5, 11, 16.75, 18 and 25mm (8x4 ft), and suits kitchen shutters, bathroom vanities and humid interiors. See HDHMR boards for how it compares with plywood.

TurBo+ is Motherwood's highest-density MDF, stocked in 3, 5.5, 8, 12 and 18mm. Boilo refers to boiling-water resistance: a Boilo board is made to survive boiling-water exposure, which is why TurBo+ is used for CNC-profiled shutters and demanding, humid positions.
