Which door to use in a bathroom

A bathroom door gets splashed, sits in steam and stands in water at the bottom edge. An MR (moisture resistant) door is made for dry rooms and swells and peels there. For a bathroom, use a BWP (boiling-water-proof) flush door, bonded with phenol-formaldehyde resin so the core does not come apart when it gets wet.

The bathroom doors we keep in stock are Marllex A grade PF (100% BWP) flush doors, Gurjan face on a pinewood core, in 30mm and 35mm.

📐 Fitting a bathroom door so it lasts

  • Seal every edge. Paint, polish or laminate all six faces. The bottom edge and any edge you plane on site matter most.
  • Keep a gap at the floor. Leave a small clearance above the finished floor so the door does not stand in water.
  • 30mm or 35mm? 30mm is the usual bathroom door. 35mm is heavier and more solid, and suits taller openings and premium fit-outs.
  • Frame: a wet frame will rot behind a waterproof door. Use a frame suited to wet areas and seal it too.
