📘 Comprehensive Product Overview

Marllex Premium Plywood represents the pinnacle of strength and longevity in the wood industry. Utilizing alternate layers of hand-selected Gurjan and hardwood core veneers, Marllex is bonded with 100% phenol formaldehyde resin. This heavy-duty construction makes it highly resilient and capable of bearing extreme loads without structural sagging or cracking.

📐 Technical Specifications & Standards

It undergoes rigorous multi-stage calibration to achieve a perfectly uniform thickness across the sheet, crucial for high-end modular furniture. The plywood is chemically treated with Glue Line Poisoning (GLP) technology, offering absolute protection against termites, borers, and dry rot.

📏 Marllex Plywood in the Rajkot Range

Marllex is one of the branded plywood lines Mahadev Traders stocks as an authorised dealer, alongside Zevik and Simson. Branded stock matters most on projects where sheets are pressed with laminate or cut on a machine, because a batch that varies in thickness or density shows up in the finished unit.

The Marllex premium plywood held in Rajkot runs 6mm, 9mm, 12mm and 18mm, across 6x3, 6x4, 7x3, 7x4, 8x3 and 8x4 ft sheets, on a Gurjan face. Cores are red-and-red, red-and-white alternate, and a 15-ply red-and-white construction in the heavier boards.

Marllex is the one branded line here stocked in both grades: MR (semi-waterproof) for dry interior furniture, and PF, 100% BWP — phenol-formaldehyde bonded, boiling water proof — for kitchen carcasses, sink runs, bathroom vanities and any position where the panel will meet water. Specify which one you need when you order; the same brand and thickness covers both.

📐 Choosing a Thickness

For wardrobe and cabinet carcasses 18mm is the usual choice; 12mm suits shelving and drawer sides; 6mm and 9mm are backing and panel infill. Sheet size is worth choosing as deliberately as thickness — starting from a 6x3 or 7x3 closer to the finished part cuts waste against cutting everything from 8x4.

If the panel is going into a dry bedroom or living room and budget is tight, the MR grade does the same job for less — see the commercial MR plywood page. Paying for the BWP grade makes sense where water is genuinely a risk.
