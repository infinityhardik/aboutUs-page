📖 Flush Door Buying Guide — Avoid Costly Mistakes

  • Never use MR doors in bathrooms — This is the most common mistake. MR-grade doors swell within 6–12 months in bathroom humidity. Always insist on BWP grade.
  • Check the frame quality — A flush door is only as good as its frame. Pine wood frames offer excellent strength and screw-holding capacity. Avoid hollow-core doors for exterior use.
  • Match thickness to purpose — 25mm for internal rooms, 30mm for main entrances, 32–35mm for heavy commercial use and main gates.
  • Plan for hardware — Ensure hinges, locks, and handles are compatible with your door thickness. We can advise on the right hardware pairing.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions About Flush Doors

A flush door has a completely flat, smooth surface on both sides, created by bonding face veneers or laminates onto a solid or semi-solid core. Unlike panel doors (which have raised or sunken panels), flush doors offer a clean, modern look ideal for contemporary interiors. They are lighter, more affordable, and easier to customise with laminates or paint.
For bathrooms, we recommend Marllex BWP flush doors or Simson BWP doors. Both brands use genuine boiling water proof adhesives and hardwood frames that resist swelling even in Gujarat's humid monsoon season. The key is to ensure the door is truly BWP-grade (not just marketed as waterproof) and has proper edge sealing.
The most common size in Gujarat's residential construction is 6 feet 6 inches × 2 feet 6 inches (6'6" × 2'6") for internal rooms and 7' × 3' for main entrances. Bathroom doors are typically smaller at 6'6" × 2' or 6'6" × 2'3". We stock all standard sizes and can supply custom dimensions for non-standard openings.
